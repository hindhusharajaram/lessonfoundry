Photosynthesis is the fundamental biological process by which autotrophic organisms, such as green plants, algae, and cyanobacteria, convert solar light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. The overall chemical equation for oxygenic photosynthesis is: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2. Through this process, plants consume carbon dioxide and water to synthesize six-carbon sugar molecules while releasing molecular oxygen as a vital byproduct.

Photosynthesis takes place within specialized cellular organelles known as chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain an internal membrane system organized into disc-like sacs called thylakoids, which are arranged in stacks known as grana. The fluid-filled space surrounding the thylakoids is called the stroma. The thylakoid membranes contain chlorophyll, the primary green pigment responsible for absorbing blue and red wavelengths of light while reflecting green light.

The photosynthetic process is divided into two interdependent stages: the light-dependent reactions and the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle). The light-dependent reactions occur across the thylakoid membrane. When photons strike chlorophyll molecules in Photosystem II, electrons are excited to higher energy levels and transferred along an electron transport chain. To replace lost electrons, water molecules undergo photolysis, splitting into protons, electrons, and diatomic oxygen gas. The electron transport chain pumps hydrogen ions across the thylakoid membrane, creating an electrochemical proton gradient. This proton motive force drives ATP synthase to generate adenosine triphosphate (ATP), while the final electron acceptor NADP+ is reduced to NADPH by ferredoxin-NADP+ reductase.

The second stage, the light-independent reactions or Calvin cycle, takes place in the chloroplast stroma. The Calvin cycle uses the chemical energy carriers ATP and NADPH generated during the light reactions to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into carbohydrates. The key enzyme catalyzing the initial carbon fixation step is ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase, commonly known as RuBisCO. RuBisCO attaches carbon dioxide to a five-carbon sugar named ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), forming an unstable six-carbon intermediate that immediately splits into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA). Through subsequent reduction steps powered by ATP and NADPH, 3-PGA is converted into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P). For every three molecules of CO2 fixed, one net molecule of G3P exits the cycle to form glucose and other organic compounds, while the remaining G3P molecules are recycled to regenerate RuBP.

Environmental factors strongly modulate the rate of photosynthesis. Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and ambient temperature act as primary limiting factors. Below the light saturation point, increasing light intensity directly accelerates photosynthetic output until the electron transport machinery reaches maximum operational capacity.
